# Reading principles for the retrieval experiment

These are the reader's working directions for Chora, not a claim to settle
Plato's meaning or a universal checklist for proving esoteric intent.

## Attention before interpretation

- Begin with exact wording. A suggested significance must have an identifiable
  textual basis, not merely a broad association such as everything happening in time.
- Attend to speaker, audience, setting, action and sequence. Do not identify a
  character's assertion with Plato's doctrine. Do not invent speaker metadata.
- Speech and action can illuminate or complicate one another. Show the actual
  text before proposing what the relationship might mean.
- Compare repetition with variation: preserve both passages and the precise
  difference in wording or circumstances.
- Preserve apparent contradictions as questions; do not automatically reconcile
  them or declare that they intentionally conceal a teaching.
- An omission requires an identifiable comparison or an explicit expectation.
  Anything unsaid is not thereby significant.
- Move between particulars and the work as a whole. A pattern must remain
  answerable to its actual occurrences and differences.
- Leave discovery to the reader. Offer a possible place to examine, not a
  verdict about what the reader must see or what Plato secretly intended.

## Scope of this first experiment

Retrieve explicit vocabulary and clear local juxtapositions in the current
canonical work. Time is the first trial topic; money is a useful contrast.
Dictionary senses and lemma-form mappings are linguistic evidence, not proof
of thematic significance. Ordinary verb tense alone need not be striking.
Preserve ambiguous analyses and incomplete occurrence coverage.

The model proposes Greek headwords. The host checks dictionary availability and
retrieves their supplied occurrences, then returns bounded canonical excerpts.
The model must inspect those results before suggesting relevance. It must not
claim a conceptual search is exhaustive or infer absence from a missing word.

Present up to three candidates with a location, exact Greek, the observable
signal and a restrained reason it may be worth examining. The model selects
retrieved candidate IDs and suggests short reasons; Chora supplies the location
and Greek from source receipts so the model cannot rewrite them.
Distinguish incidental references from potentially conspicuous combinations.
Translations and reasons remain model suggestions and need reader review.

Visions, reader notes, theme marking, cross-work patterns and independent
interpretation are outside this trial. Future verb-form exploration is a
separate capability. Broader comparisons require a specific reader request.

## Background to the reading approach

- [Strauss and esoteric writing](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/strauss-leo/).
- [Melzer on pedagogical esotericism](https://econjwatch.org/file_download/1060/MelzerMay2018.pdf).
- [Plato's Phaedrus, including 275d–276a](https://www.platonicfoundation.org/media/2025/06/platos-phaedrus-english-translation-by-david-horan.pdf).

These motivate careful attention and reader participation. They do not license
an automatic hidden-meaning detector. Practical acceptance depends on the
passages the experiment actually returns and the reader's assessment of them.
